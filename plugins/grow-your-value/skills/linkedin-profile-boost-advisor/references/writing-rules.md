# Writing rules for the new copy

Read at step 6 and before the check in step 8. The copy is the owner's voice: plain simple English, short sentences, first person, concrete nouns. These rules are written for this skill from common editing practice and the sources in `references/research.md`; no text is copied from them.

## The outcome pattern

An output says what you did. An outcome says what changed because of it, for someone who cares: money, time, users, risk, people.

`Problem or gap → what you did → what changed → one number, with its source`

| Output (before, shape only) | Outcome (after, shape only) |
|---|---|
| Led development of an invoicing platform | Finance closed the month in 2 days instead of 9, because invoices stopped needing manual matching |
| Managed a team of engineers | Hired and grew two teams to 14 people; the first release shipped six weeks after the second team started |
| Responsible for architecture | Moved three services to one stack; on-call pages fell from 40 to 12 a month |

The examples above are invented to show the shape. Use the owner's own facts only.

Rules for each bullet:

- One number per claim, and it must have a source on the facts sheet. A number the owner cannot source is cut or turned into a question.
- Start with the change or the problem, not with "Responsible for", "Led", "Managed", "Spearheaded". A verb is fine when it names a specific act ("Moved", "Cut", "Hired").
- Say who benefited (a user, a customer, the team, the investors) in a noun, not an adjective.
- Real names where the owner confirms they are public; otherwise "a Berlin health-tech startup".
- Keep one consistent tense: the current role in present, old roles in past.

## Patterns to avoid

The script flags some of these; the rest you catch by reading.

- Lists of three adjectives or three abstract nouns ("scalable, reliable and secure"). One concrete detail beats three words. A list of tools is fine.
- The rhythm of "Not X, but Y", "It's not about A. It's about B."
- Dashes as punctuation between clauses; titles built from a chain of dashes or pipes with no content between them. Keep at most one dash in About.
- Openers that announce: "Passionate about", "I'm thrilled to", "In today's fast-paced world", "Proven track record".
- Hype and filler words: leverage, unlock, empower, spearhead, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changing, synergy, innovative, transformative, dynamic, results-driven, visionary.
- Claims with no scene: "transforming startups into scalable tech companies". Replace with the one startup, the number, and what changed.
- Questions as hooks, emoji as bullets, hashtags in About, all-caps words.
- Smooth, even sentences all the same length. Mix short and long; one plain sentence in a row is fine.

## Voice

Write the way the owner would explain it to a smart stranger at a meetup. Use contractions. Keep one honest, small human detail if the owner gave one. If the owner's own writing is available (a post, an email), match its vocabulary and do not polish it into native-sounding perfection.

## Before you hand over

Read each bullet aloud. If you could swap in another person's name and it would still be true, it is too generic: add the scene.
