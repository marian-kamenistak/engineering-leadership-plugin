# Section playbook

Read at steps 4, 5 and 7. Limits are the ones reported for 2026 and change without notice: confirm on LinkedIn's edit screen before you quote one to the owner. Statements about how LinkedIn ranks profiles come from practitioner reports and LinkedIn's own talent blog, not from a published algorithm; write "recruiters search by" and "reported", never "the algorithm does".

## Who reads, in order

1. A recruiter or a sourcing tool types a title and a few keywords. Titles and keywords are separate fields in recruiter search, so the target title must be in the headline and a role title, not only in prose.
2. A hiring manager opens the profile for about ninety seconds: photo, headline, the first lines of About, the current role, Featured.
3. A screening tool or a careful recruiter compares the profile with the CV. Dates, titles and numbers must match.

## Limits (reported, 2026)

| Field | Limit | What shows first |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | 220 characters | about 70 in search results and on mobile |
| About | 2,600 characters | about 200 to 300 before "see more" |
| Role description | 2,000 characters | first lines |
| Skills | 100 | the 3 pinned ones |
| Custom URL | 3 to 100 characters | all |
| Post | 3,000 characters | about 200 before "see more" |

## Headline

Formula: `<target title> | <the one specialty> | <one proof>`. Put the title and the specialty inside the first 70 characters. One proof, one number, with its source on the facts sheet. Keep "ex-" names and stacks of buzzwords out. If the person leads a company stage, say the stage ("seed to Series A") rather than "scalable".

Shape only: `Head of Engineering | Fintech platforms | Cut release time from 3 weeks to 2 days`

Never leave the default "Title at Company" when the person is looking.

## About

Four short paragraphs, first person, blank lines between them (most views are on a phone):

1. The hook, 200 characters: who you help and the result, in one or two sentences. This is all most readers see.
2. Two or three stories, one paragraph each: the problem, what you did, what changed, one number. Lead with the story the reader would not guess.
3. How you work with a team, in one paragraph, with an example instead of adjectives.
4. What you are looking for and how to reach you: the role, the place, the contact route (a domain email or a booking link).

Target length 1,200 to 1,800 characters. Length is not the goal; every paragraph must hold a fact.

## Experience: outputs to outcomes

Pattern and examples in `references/writing-rules.md`. Per role: one line of scope (team size, budget, users, stage), then two to four bullets. The current and target-matching roles get the most. Roles older than ten years get one line each; if a role is off-lane and short (a few months), recommend removing it and say what the gap looks like with and without it.

Titles must stay true. A title may carry a clarifying tag only when the owner confirms it is accurate ("CTO (founding team)").

## Featured

Three to five items, in this order: the strongest proof people can open (a press article, a published paper, a case study, a talk), then a link to a personal page that carries more, then one post if it is good. Buried stories live here: a press feature, an academic publication, an audit or review the person took part in, a TV or podcast appearance. Each item needs a title that says the outcome and a one-line caption.

## Skills

Pin three: the target title's function first, the strongest technical area second, the most requested domain third. Keep 20 to 30 skills; remove ones that point to a different lane or to technology the person no longer works with. Recommend asking for endorsements on the pinned three only, from people who saw the work.

## Photo and banner

Photo: a clear head-and-shoulders picture, face large, plain background, recent, same person as in the CV and on the personal site. The banner carries the lane in six words or fewer, or the logo of a place or product the person is known for. A CV should carry the same photo as the profile where the local norm allows it (common in Germany).

## Custom URL and contact

`linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname`, no numbers. In Contact info: one website the owner controls, and one email on an own domain; a free-mail address on a leadership profile reads as unfinished. If the domain does not exist yet, "register it and set up the mailbox" is a "do first" item.

## Recommendations to ask for

Two or three, each from a named person who saw a named result: a former manager, a founder or CEO who worked with the person, a peer from a hard project. Write the one-sentence ask the owner can send, in their voice, naming the project and the point to cover ("the week we moved the billing system to the new stack"). Offer to write a draft for the recommender to edit; never write one that is posted as theirs without their edit.

## Open to work

For a discreet search, recruiters-only visibility; for an open one, titles and places set to the lane. Check the setting matches the target (location, remote, titles).

## 2-week posting starter

Four posts in 14 days (for example day 1, 4, 8, 12), 100 to 250 words, first person, one story each. Why: activity in the last 30 days is reported to help visibility, and a profile with no post since years reads as dormant; the aim is a first rhythm, not a content plan.

1. The buried story: the thing the reader would not guess (press, paper, an unusual product), with a link in the first comment or Featured.
2. A result post: one outcome from the last role, one number, one thing you would do differently.
3. A lesson from leading a team: a decision, what you chose, what it cost.
4. What you are looking for: the role, the place, the date, and who to write to.

For each: the first line written out (under 150 characters, no question hook, no "I'm thrilled"), a three-line outline, and the fact it rests on. The owner writes the body; offer to draft it in the same plain style from the outline.

## Keyword words for engineering leadership (starter list, not a measurement)

Titles: CTO, VP Engineering, Head of Engineering, Engineering Director, Director of Engineering, Head of Development, Engineering Manager. Domain: technical due diligence, scaling engineering teams, platform architecture, hiring, engineering strategy, fundraising support, product engineering. Add the owner's industry and two tools. Prefer the words in the target ads when the owner has them.
