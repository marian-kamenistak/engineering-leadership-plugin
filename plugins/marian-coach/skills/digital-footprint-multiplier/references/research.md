# Research: where the evidence lives

Read this before step 2 of the workflow. The aim is evidence a stranger could find in ten minutes, because that is what a recruiter, a hiring manager or a screening tool works with.

## 1. Confirm identity before anything counts

A name is not a person. Accept a page as theirs only with two matching signals: the same employer, the same city, the same photo, or a link to it from their LinkedIn (Contact info, Featured, About). A GitHub account that merely shares the name scores nothing. List the look-alikes you left out under "What I could not see".

## 2. LinkedIn, section by section

| Section | What to copy | Feeds |
|---|---|---|
| Headline, About | the exact text | headline |
| Experience | two or three lines per recent role, team sizes, dates | outcomes, scope, readability |
| Featured, Contact info | every outgoing link | proof of work, voice |
| Skills | the top ten, and whether they match the roles | readability |
| Recommendations | who wrote each one (manager, report, peer), the date, the first sentence | third-party or people proof |
| Activity, last 12 months | their own posts: how many and how often (posts a month), topics, who answers | voice, AI fluency, recency |
| Followers, connections ("500+") | the numbers | network |

## 3. The open web

Search the full name in quotes with one qualifier at a time: employer, city, `github`, `talk`, `podcast`, `meetup`, `conference`, the main technology, `interview`.

| Look for | Where | Feeds |
|---|---|---|
| Code | GitHub and GitLab profile: pinned repositories, READMEs, stars, forks, contributions in the last year; npm, PyPI, crates, Docker Hub | proof of work, recency |
| Shipped things | a product page, an app store entry, a demo, a company engineering blog with their byline | proof of work, outcomes |
| AI in their work | a published skill, agent, MCP server or prompt set; posts that describe a task given to an agent | AI fluency, AI leadership |
| Voice | YouTube and conference pages, Sessionize, meetup pages, podcast episodes, their blog, Medium, dev.to, Substack | voice |
| Other people | mentions in posts and articles, awards, thanks in release notes, co-authored work | third-party proof |
| Research and inventions | scientific papers (Google Scholar, arXiv, ResearchGate, DBLP) and patents (Google Patents, Espacenet) under their name; count them only with the identity check passed | proof of work, third-party proof, technical credibility |
| Their own site | a personal web page or blog: what it says they do, how fresh it is, whether it agrees with LinkedIn | headline, voice, fresh and consistent |
| Community | meetup organiser pages, open-source maintainer lists, community member pages | network |
| For managers | hiring posts, team pages, talks about how they run teams, press about the organisation | scope, outcomes, people proof |

## 4. Paid research

Use paid tools when plain search stalls: a scraper for a page that blocks fetching, a search API for more results, a deep-research tool for people with a long public history. Before the first paid call, check that the user allowed it. Keep a tally (tool, number of calls) for the Method line of the report.

## 5. Evidence hygiene

- Quote lines exactly, with the URL and the read date.
- A claim on their own profile is evidence that the claim is visible, not that it is true. Score visibility.
- Old glory counts less: for voice and recency look at the last 12 months; for proof of work a project can be older if people still use it.
- When two sources disagree (LinkedIn says 12 people, a talk says 30), note it: consistency is part of "Fresh and consistent".
- Do not log into anything on the person's behalf, and do not message anyone to ask about them.
